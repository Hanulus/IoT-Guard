# User Interviews (24 October 2026)

Five potential users were interviewed by the student. Answers were collected in writing (chat messages and short notes) and translated from Russian into English. Names are not recorded; each person has a code U1 to U5.

## Questions (the same for everyone)

1. Which smart devices do you have at home or in the office? (camera, speaker, plugs, Smart TV, vacuum, etc.)
2. Do you know what these devices send to the internet? Have you ever checked?
3. Has any device behaved strangely? (slow network, unknown logins, camera moving by itself)
4. If one of your devices was hacked, how would you like to find out: just "device X is suspicious" or with details? And where: Telegram, push notification, web page?
5. How many false alarms are acceptable for you: once a day, once a week or once a month?
6. Would you put a small box near the router (like a Raspberry Pi) or would you prefer a program on your computer?

## U1

1. Smart TV, smart speaker and a robot vacuum.
2. Understands that they are online and send something, but never checked what exactly.
3. The TV sometimes takes long to connect to Wi-Fi, nothing really suspicious.
4. A notification that says what the problem is, for example in Telegram, so it is seen at once.
5. Once a month is fine. With daily messages they would stop paying attention.
6. Probably a box near the router, if it works by itself and does not need attention.

## U2

1. Wi-Fi camera, TV and smart plugs.
2. Knows they work through the internet, never checked what is sent.
3. The camera sometimes turns off by itself; usually just restarts it without finding the reason.
4. Push notification on the phone, preferably with an explanation. A message like "something is wrong" does not say much.
5. Once a week is still fine, more often would be annoying.
6. A program on the computer. Does not want to buy another device.

## U3

1. Smart speaker and an Android TV.
2. Never checked. Understands that some data is sent, but has no idea what.
3. No, everything works normally.
4. A short alert that a specific device has a problem is enough, with details available if interested. Telegram would be good.
5. Once a week, as long as these are real problems and not messages about every small thing.
6. Rather a box near the router: install once and forget, if it really works automatically.

## U4

1. Robot vacuum, Smart TV and smart bulbs.
2. Heard that such devices collect data, but never looked at what they send.
3. The vacuum sometimes loses connection with its app; does not know if it is Wi-Fi or something else.
4. Wants a clear explanation: which device, what happened and whether any action is needed. A phone notification is enough.
5. Better once a month. Frequent mistakes would quickly become annoying.
6. A program on the computer, seems simpler and nothing extra to buy.

## U5

1. Security camera, smart speaker and a PS5.
2. Understands they connect to servers and exchange data, but never followed it on purpose.
3. The camera once stopped working through the app and had to be reconnected; not sure if it was related to security.
4. A message with the device name and an explanation of the problem. Checks Telegram often, so Telegram is convenient.
5. Once a week at most. But if someone is really trying to hack a device, the number of alerts does not matter.
6. A box near the router, if it checks everything by itself and does not need constant setup.
